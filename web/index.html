How it works

Three things, and the third
is the unusual one.

  1. 01

    The vault is a file on your Mac

    Encrypted with a key derived from your master password and a 256-bit account key that is generated once and never transmitted. Lose both and the data is gone — that is the design working, not a bug.

  2. 02

    Sync is optional, and blind

    Devices exchange signed, encrypted changes through a relay. Every change is sealed before it leaves and hash-chained on arrival, so a relay that is compromised — or compelled — can withhold data but cannot read or forge it.

  3. 03

    The extension runs on no page until you ask

    Most managers request access to every site you visit, forever. This one asks the browser for the page only when you invoke it, and the origin it matches against comes from the browser rather than from the page — so a lookalike site cannot claim to be your bank.

Agent Guard · in design

A key you paste is a key
you have published.

Secrets end up in chat boxes, issue trackers and prompts because that is where the work is. Mortise recognises one of yours on its way out and kills it at the provider, in the seconds before anyone reads the message.

Revoking is the part that always works — every provider will let you burn a key. Some go further and let a replacement be minted in the same breath, and where that is true the new one lands in your vault before you have switched windows.

Issuing a replacement needs a credential that can issue keys. It lives in your vault, on your Mac, and the call goes straight from your machine to the provider — no server of ours is ever in the path.

Sharing · in design

Give someone a credential
without giving it to a server.

Shared vaults work the way the private one does. The key is wrapped to each member’s device, the invite link carries it in the part of the URL a browser never sends, and membership is a signed log every device checks for itself.

  • The relay cannot read the vault

    Not as a policy, as a consequence. It holds sealed changes and the signatures over them, and no key that opens either. Tools that keep your secrets server-side can only promise not to look.

  • The invite never reaches us

    The key rides in the link’s fragment — the part after the #, which browsers keep local and never put in a request. Send the link however you like; the relay sees a join, not a key.

  • Removing someone rotates the key

    A new key is issued to everyone who remains, and everything written afterwards is sealed to it. What they already synced is theirs — no amount of cryptography reaches into someone else’s disk, and the app says so when you remove them.

Limits

What this does not protect you from.

Every password manager has these. Most do not print them on the marketing page. The full version is in the threat model, which is in the repository and was written before the code.

  • Malware running as you

    Root access or code injected into the app wins. No user-space password manager survives this, and any that claims to is lying.

  • A keylogger during unlock

    Biometric unlock narrows the window after the first unlock. It does not close it.

  • The relay learning who syncs with whom

    Contents are sealed; the fact that two devices talked, and when, is not. Minimised, not eliminated.

  • Losing both your password and account key

    There is no reset link. This is the cost of nobody else holding a key, and it is not recoverable by us or by anyone.

  • Someone you shared with keeping a copy

    Revoking rotates the keys going forward. It cannot reach back into what they already synced.

  • A file that leaks its own shape

    The local database is plain SQLite with encrypted fields, so an attacker holding the file learns how many items exist and roughly how large each one is — never what they say.

Status

Not shipped yet.

The vault, sync, the browser extension and import from other managers all work. There is no signed build to download, which means no Touch ID and no Chrome Web Store listing until there is. Everything is in the open while it gets there.
